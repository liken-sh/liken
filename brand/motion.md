# Motion

These rules govern the `liken` mark in motion, wherever a surface
animates it: an idle screen, a site, an installer. The mark is the
fourteen-hexagon mosaic in `liken.svg`, and its motion is one loop
with one control. The last section governs the focus outline, the
stroke that shows which item a press of select acts on.

## The loop

Each hexagon pulses in size about its own center, up to ten percent
at full energy. Each hexagon uses its own phase and rate, so the
fourteen tiles move independently. The independent pulses are intended
to give the mark an organic appearance. The offsets derive from each
hexagon's index, not from a random source, so the same moment renders
the same way on every run and on every surface.

## The energy

One value, called energy, ranges from zero to one and controls the
loop. At zero the mark is still. This is its resting state; do not
animate it for decoration. Energy controls both the size of each
pulse and its speed. As energy decreases, the pulses become smaller
and slower. Ease every change in energy rather than changing it
abruptly.

## What motion means

The mark moves only while the system performs work that a person
waits for. Ramp the energy up as the work starts, hold it while the
work runs in view, and ease it back to zero as the work ends. Keep the
mark still at rest, so motion indicates work in progress.

## The focus outline

The focus outline is not the mark, and the rules above do not apply to
it. It moves on every change of focus, because a person on a couch
looks for focus after each press. When focus moves, the outline
travels from where it is drawn to the item that takes focus. The edge
on the side of travel arrives first and the opposite edge follows, so
for a moment the outline spans both items and then closes on the new
one. A row or a grid that scrolls on the same press eases to rest in
the same time, and the outline goes to where the item rests, so the
two land together. Nothing overshoots. When presses arrive faster than
the motion, as from a held key, the motion shortens so the outline
never falls behind the remote. A change of page moves no outline: it
appears on the new page's focus.

While focus rests, the outline breathes. A few seconds after the last
motion, its color brightens past the full accent, toward the text
color, and returns, slowly and at long intervals. Only the color
changes. A press returns the outline to its resting color in a
fraction of a second. The outline holds still under a sleeping screen
and under a film, because nobody sees it there.
